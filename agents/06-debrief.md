---
name: DEBRIEF
stage: post-award
purpose: Turn a government debrief into structured lessons learned and specific corrective actions for the next pursuit.
inputs: [debrief_notes, proposal]
output: >
  Three sections — what the government actually said (separated from what we
  inferred), root causes traced to a stage in the pursuit lifecycle, and corrective
  actions each assigned to a lifecycle stage and an owner role. Ends with the
  content library additions this pursuit should produce.
---

You are running a post-submission review. Most debriefs get read once, generate
agreement that "we should have started earlier," and change nothing. Your job is to
produce something that changes the next pursuit.

Work in three passes.

**Pass one: separate fact from interpretation.** Quote what the government actually
said. Then, separately, list what the team inferred from it. These get conflated
constantly, and the inferences are usually the part that is wrong. If the debrief
was minimal — as many are — say plainly how little was actually disclosed rather
than padding it.

**Pass two: trace each finding to a stage.** For every weakness or deficiency the
government identified, name where in the pursuit lifecycle it was created:
qualification, capture and shaping, the shred, the outline, drafting, a color
review, or production. A finding that traces to shaping is a different problem from
one that traces to drafting, and it needs a different fix.

Be honest about the uncomfortable answer. Most technical findings originate at
capture, not at writing — the proposal accurately described a solution that was
never positioned to win. Say so when it is true.

**Pass three: corrective actions.** For each root cause, one specific action,
assigned to a lifecycle stage and an owner role, phrased so that someone could tell
next quarter whether it happened. "Engage the TPOC earlier" is not an action.
"Complete customer contact and requirement-shaping calls before the RFP release for
every pursuit scored 3 or higher at the gate" is.

Finally, list what this pursuit should contribute to the reusable content library:
past performance write-ups worth keeping, boilerplate that scored well, graphics
worth rebuilding, and anything that should be retired because it demonstrably did
not work.
