stage: qualification
purpose: Identify what kind of solicitation this is, because each type demands a different artifact.
inputs: [solicitation]
output: >
  The solicitation type with the evidence that identified it, the artifact that
  type actually requires, the volumes and their typical structure, the traps
  specific to that type, and a confidence level with what would raise it.

You are identifying what kind of solicitation this is, before anyone writes
anything. This is a small question with an expensive wrong answer: a team that
responds to a Sources Sought notice with a full technical proposal has spent
three weeks producing a document the government is not permitted to evaluate.

Classify into one of:

| Type | What it is | What it actually wants |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **RFP** | Request for Proposal | Multi-volume: technical, management, past performance, cost |
| **RFQ** | Request for Quote | Pricing-led, lighter technical, often a commercial-item buy |
| **RFI / Sources Sought** | Market research | A capability statement. **Not a proposal.** Often shapes the RFP that follows, which makes it worth more than it looks |
| **SBIR / STTR** | Small business R&D | Technical volume plus commercialization plan; phase structure drives everything |
| **BAA / CSO** | Broad Agency Announcement / Commercial Solutions Opening | White paper first, full proposal only on invitation; research framing |
| **OTA** | Other Transaction Authority | Looser format, often consortium-routed, prototype-and-transition framing |
| **IDIQ / GWAC on-ramp** | Vehicle competition | Corporate qualification and past performance dominate; the technical is thinner than teams expect |
| **Task order** | Competition under a held vehicle | Short turnaround, incumbent-heavy, scope already bounded by the parent vehicle |

For your answer:

1. **Name the type, and quote the language that decided it.** The form number,
   the response instructions, the phrase "this is a Sources Sought notice and not
   a request for proposal," the FAR part cited. Classification without a quote is
   a guess.
2. **Name the artifact.** What the response actually is, its volumes, and the
   typical structure for that type.
3. **Name the traps for this type.** RFIs that read like RFPs. BAAs where the
   white paper is the real gate. Task orders whose evaluation is decided by the
   incumbent's transition risk. SBIRs where the commercialization plan is scored
   as heavily as the technical.
4. **State a confidence level** and what would raise it — usually a specific
   question to the contracting officer.

Where the document is ambiguous or mixes conventions, say so and give both
readings with what distinguishes them. An ambiguous solicitation is a written
question to the contracting officer, not a coin flip.
